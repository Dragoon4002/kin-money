KIN — 2-MINUTE HACKATHON PITCH
Target duration: ~1:50–2:00. Keep pace brisk; skip details if the demo runs long.

SLIDE 1 — MONEY THAT BEHAVES
Kin is a mobile-first money app for people who care not just where money goes, but what it should do next.

SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM
The World Bank estimated India’s remittance inflows at $129 billion in 2024. Across the world, sending $200 still costs an average 6.36% in the latest surfaced Q3 2025 benchmark. But the problem is bigger than a transfer fee: once money lands, the sender’s intent often disappears.

SLIDE 3 — THE IDEA
Kin turns a payment into a promise. Instead of only sending money, families can attach rules: release an allowance over time, pool a family pot, or save together toward a goal.

SLIDE 4 — THE EXPERIENCE
The product aims to feel familiar: sign in with a passkey, send to a person, and get a clear receipt. Blockchain details stay backstage. These are illustrative UI mockups, not screenshots.

SLIDE 5 — WHAT MONEY CAN DO
The current prototype explores allowances, emergency releases, savings circles, family pots, earmarked payments and milestone escrow. These programmable flows are testnet or practice-money features, not claims of production-ready mainnet functionality.

SLIDE 6 — THE RAILS
Kin uses a Next.js app, Mera’s passkey account layer, smart contracts on Monad and Agora AUSD, with gasless relaying and supporting indexing and notifications. Mainnet currently supports add money, send/receive and activity. Two add-money conversions were observed: 4.997 USDC to 4.998 AUSD, and 0.999 USDC to 0.999 AUSD. INR cash-out is only a labelled practice step; live INR payout is not available today.

SLIDE 7 — EARLY EVIDENCE
Six independent testers beyond the founder are documented; five used a feature beyond signup. There are two observed mainnet add-money conversions. This is early evidence only — not proof of retention, scale or product-market fit.

SLIDE 8 — ROADMAP AND CTA
The next steps are to harden core flows, measure repeat use, and build a compliant INR exit. We’re looking for a connection to a licensed INR payout partner and the relevant approvals. Kin’s bet is simple: money should keep its promise after you press send.
